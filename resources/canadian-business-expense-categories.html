Canadian Business Expense Categories: A Complete List for Tax Season

This is a reference guide for Canadian small businesses filing their T2 (corporation) or T1 (self-employed) business income. The categories below align with how CRA categorises deductible business expenses, with GST/HST (ITC) treatment noted for each.

Advertising and Marketing

Includes online advertising (Google Ads, Meta Ads), print advertising, website costs, marketing agency fees, sponsorships, and promotional materials.

ITC: Fully eligible. Claim HST paid on all advertising costs. CRA note: Yellow Pages-style directory fees and business card printing are included. Costs to run a personal social media profile are not eligible unless clearly for business purposes.

Business Meals and Entertainment

Client dinners, team meals, sports events for client entertainment, and similar expenses.

CRA rule: Only 50% of eligible meal and entertainment expenses are deductible. Keep a record of who attended and the business purpose. ITC: You can claim ITCs on 100% of the HST paid, but your income deduction is limited to 50%.

Professional and Legal Fees

Accountant fees, lawyer fees, consulting fees, bookkeeping, and other professional services.

ITC: Fully eligible if the service provider is registered for GST/HST and includes their registration number on the invoice. CRA note: Legal fees related to personal matters are not eligible even if paid from a business account.

Office Supplies and Expenses

Printer paper, pens, postage, courier fees, office furniture (under $500 per item), software subscriptions.

ITC: Fully eligible. CRA note: Small equipment under your CRA threshold may be fully expensed in the year; above the threshold, items become capital assets subject to CCA.

Vehicle Expenses

Fuel, insurance, repairs, parking, vehicle registration, leasing costs, and car washes.

ITC: Eligible on the business-use portion only. You must maintain a mileage log to establish the business-use percentage. CRA note: Without a mileage log, CRA can disallow all vehicle expense claims. The log must show date, destination, purpose, and distance for each business trip.

Travel Expenses

Hotels, flights, car rentals, meals while travelling for business, and local transit costs.

Home Office Expenses

For businesses where the owner works from home. Eligible expenses include a proportional share of rent, mortgage interest, utilities, internet, and home insurance.

ITC: Eligible on the business-use portion (calculated as a percentage of home square footage used exclusively for business). CRA note: The home office must be your principal place of business, or a space used exclusively and regularly for meeting clients.

Salaries, Wages, and Contractor Payments

Amounts paid to employees (salaries, bonuses, overtime) and independent contractors.

Bank Fees and Interest

Business bank account fees, credit card annual fees (on a business card), and interest on business loans or lines of credit.

Insurance

Business liability insurance, commercial property insurance, key person life insurance, professional liability (E&O).

Software and Technology

SaaS subscriptions, cloud storage, phone plans (business portion), computer equipment, software licences.
