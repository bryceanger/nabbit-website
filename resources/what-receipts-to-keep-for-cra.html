What Receipts Do You Need to Keep for CRA?

If the Canada Revenue Agency audits your business, the burden of proof is on you. Expenses that cannot be substantiated with appropriate documentation are denied — and if the amounts are material, so are the GST/HST Input Tax Credits (ITCs) associated with them.

The Short Answer

CRA requires that you keep supporting documentation for all business expenses you deduct from income and all GST/HST ITCs you claim. A receipt is required if the expense is over $30. For expenses under $30, CRA permits a log entry — but a receipt is still best practice.

What Makes a Receipt Valid for CRA?

A valid business receipt must include:

  1. Vendor name and address (or sufficient information to identify the supplier)
  2. Date of the transaction
  3. Amount paid (including the amount before tax)
  4. GST/HST registration number of the vendor (required for ITC claims on transactions $150+)
  5. Description of the goods or services purchased
  6. Amount of GST/HST charged (for transactions over $30 where you are claiming an ITC)

For transactions between $30 and $149.99, CRA requires items 1–5. For transactions $150 and over, CRA requires all six fields including the HST registration number.

Full vs. Simplified Receipts — The CRA Rules

CRA distinguishes between simplified invoices (under $150) and full invoices ($150 and over):

Simplified invoice (transactions under $150): Vendor name, invoice date, total amount paid (tax included), HST/GST rate applied (e.g., 13% HST Ontario).

Full invoice (transactions $150 and over): All of the above, plus the HST/GST registration number of the vendor, and the amount before tax and tax amount listed separately.

If you want to claim the ITC on a $200 business lunch and the restaurant receipt does not show the vendor's GST/HST registration number, CRA can disallow the ITC. This is one of the most common reasons ITCs are denied on audit.

What Counts as Acceptable Documentation?

CRA accepts: original paper receipts; electronic receipts (email receipts, PDF receipts from vendors); and photographed images of paper receipts. Digital images of receipts are fully accepted by CRA, provided the image is legible and includes all required fields.

Credit card statements and bank statements alone do not qualify. They show that a payment was made — not what was purchased or whether HST was charged. CRA does not accept them as standalone ITC documentation.

The 6 Categories CRA Scrutinises Most

  1. Meals and entertainment — dust show the business purpose and who attended. CRA limits deductibility to 50% of eligible expenses.
  2. Vehicle expenses — a mileage log is required in addition to fuel and maintenance receipts. Without a log, CRA can disallow the entire vehicle expense deduction.
  3. Home office expenses — receipts for home costs need to be allocated to a business-use percentage; a T2200 or reasonable calculation is required.
  4. Client gifts — subject to the 50% entertainment rule; documentation of the business purpose is required.
  5. Travel expenses — hotel folios, airfare, car rental, and meal receipts should show dates, destinations, and business purpose.
  6. Contractor payments — invoices from contractors should show the contractor's GST/HST registration number if they are charging HST.

How Long Do You Need to Keep Business Receipts?

Six years from the end of the tax year they relate to. If your fiscal year ends December 31, 2024, you must keep 2024 receipts until at least December 31, 2030. Keep older receipts if a return is still under objection or appeal, or if the receipt relates to a capital purchase — keep those for 6 years after you sell or dispose of the asset.
