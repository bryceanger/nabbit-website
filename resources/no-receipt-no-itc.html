No Receipt, No ITC: What CRA Requires

If your business is GST/HST-registered, every dollar of GST or HST you pay on business expenses can be recovered as an Input Tax Credit — but only if you have the receipt to prove it. This is what CRA requires, and what happens when you don't have it.

The Rule

Under the Excise Tax Act, claiming an Input Tax Credit (ITC) requires that you have documentary evidence to support the claim. That evidence must exist at the time you file your GST/HST return. If CRA audits your ITC claims and you cannot produce the supporting documentation, the ITC is denied — and you may owe the amount back plus interest.

There is no appeal on the basis of "I know I paid HST on that expense." Without the receipt, the ITC does not exist.

What Documentation CRA Requires

For transactions under $30: a log entry or other notation is technically sufficient, though a receipt is best practice.

For transactions $30–$149.99 (simplified invoice): the receipt must show the vendor name, date, total amount paid (including tax), and the HST or GST rate applied.

For transactions $150 and over (full invoice): all of the above, plus the vendor's GST/HST registration number, and the tax amount shown separately from the base amount.

The most common reason ITCs are denied: a receipt for a transaction over $150 that does not show the vendor's HST registration number.

What Counts as Acceptable Documentation

CRA accepts original paper receipts, electronic receipts (email receipts, PDF invoices), and photographed images of paper receipts. A clear, legible photo of a receipt taken on a smartphone satisfies CRA's documentation requirements — provided it includes all required fields and is legible.

Bank statements and credit card statements alone do not qualify. They show that a payment was made; they do not show what was purchased or and whether HST was charged. CRA specifically states that bank statements are not acceptable as standalone ITC documentation.

The Practical Problem: Receipts Disappear

Thermal paper — used by most point-of-sale receipt printers — fades within 2–5 years under normal storage conditions. CRA requires businesses to keep records for 6 years. A 2024 restaurant receipt stored in a filing cabinet may be completely blank by 2028 — well within the audit window.

The solution is to photograph every receipt at the point of purchase and store the image digitally. A legible digital image satisfies CRA's requirements and does not degrade.
