What Is an Input Tax Credit?

An Input Tax Credit (ITC) lets a GST/HST-registered business recover the GST/HST it paid on business purchases. You claim it on your GST/HST return, and you need the supporting documents to back each claim. That is why most of these guides come back to the same point: the receipt is the evidence.

Why Do Receipts Matter So Much to CRA?

CRA expects you to hold documents that support every expense and every credit you claim, and to keep them for six years. Thermal paper receipts fade well before then, and a faded receipt is often the same as no receipt. Capturing a digital copy on the day you pay is the simplest way to protect both the deduction and the ITC.

What Should You Do This Month to Stay Audit-Ready?

  • Capture every business receipt on the day you pay, including small ones such as parking and subscriptions.
  • Check that each receipt shows the supplier name, date, amount, and GST/HST, plus the supplier's registration number on purchases of $100 or more.
  • Match each receipt to an expense category so your GST/HST return and your income tax return use the same numbers.
  • Keep digital copies for six years from the end of the tax year they relate to.
  • Review your Input Tax Credit total against your receipts before you file, so that every credit you claim has a document behind it.

How Do Receipts, ITCs, and Expense Categories Fit Together?

They answer three different questions about the same purchase. The receipt proves the purchase happened. The expense category decides how the cost appears on your income tax return and whether you can deduct all of it or only part, as with the 50% limit on meals and entertainment. The Input Tax Credit decides whether you can recover the GST/HST you paid. A single missing receipt can put all three at risk, which is why the guides above treat receipt capture as the first step rather than an afterthought.
